# Beginner usability session

Use a private browser window and fictional names and notes. Ask participants whether you may record anonymous timings and observations. Keep their research and login details out of the test.

**Scenario**. You are considering a student project about the relationship between sleep quality and academic performance. You have fifteen minutes to find a source, start a draft, and keep a recoverable copy.

| Prompt for the participant | What the observer records |
| --- | --- |
| Open Home. Explain what you could do here, then start in whichever way you prefer. | Time to choose, tour choice, help requested |
| Find a relevant paper. Narrow the search, inspect a title, and save a reference to a project. | Time to find Discover and Filters, source used, repeated clicks or confusion |
| Write a working research question. Decide whether the source fits. Record a reason, a page or section, what the source reports, and your own interpretation. | Whether the reader distinguishes the published finding from their interpretation |
| Start a paragraph, insert the linked citation, inspect the bibliography and download a draft. | Correct source, visible export, any navigation detours |
| Make an encrypted backup. Verify the downloaded file. Show where you would replay or dismiss guidance. | Whether file verification succeeds, assistance and errors |

Let the participant decide where to click. Record assistance separately from unaided success. Ask which label they would change and why. Report the number of participants, device widths, task completions and median times. Share recurring obstacles with examples. Study results require actual participants.
